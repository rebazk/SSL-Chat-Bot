## Question 1

**Question:** What is the Sustainable Solutions Lab, and what does it focus on?

**Answer:** The Sustainable Solutions Lab (SSL) at UMass Boston is a climate justice-focused institute that bridges sectors, convenes collaborators, and advances transdisciplinary research and action centered on communities facing climate change, especially communities that have been historically excluded.

**Citations**

- [https://www.umb.edu/ssl/](https://www.umb.edu/ssl/)
- [https://scholarworks.umb.edu/ssl/](https://scholarworks.umb.edu/ssl/)

## Question 2

**Question:** According to the SSL materials, what are some climate-related concerns or priorities raised by Boston communities of color?

**Answer:** Across the SSL materials, Boston communities of color raise concerns about health impacts, air quality and asthma, climate preparedness, and whether institutions are listening to community knowledge and investing equitably. The materials also stress that communities of color should have a stronger leadership role in climate responses.

**Citations**

- Voices that Matter: Boston Area Residents of Color Discuss Climate Change (p. 18)

## Question 3

**Question:** What does the report 'Who Counts in Climate Resilience?' identify as the two transient populations it focuses on?

**Answer:** The report focuses on two transient populations: people experiencing homelessness and international seasonal H-2B workers.

**Citations**

- Who Counts in Climate Resilience? Transient Populations and Climate Resilience in Boston and Cape Cod, Massachusetts (p. 22)

## Question 4

**Question:** What kinds of funding mechanisms are discussed in SSL's climate resilience financing work?

**Answer:** The financing report discusses several mechanisms, including bonds, loans or collateral-based financing, resilience fees, risk-based pricing or insurance, and funding at district, city-region, and building-parcel scales.

**Citations**

- Executive Summary: Financing Climate Resilience: Mobilizing Resources and Incentives to Protect Boston from Climate Risks (p. 6)

## Question 5

**Question:** What are some of the governance or policy changes recommended for adapting Boston's built environment to increased flooding?

**Answer:** The governance report recommends updating governance tools to support Climate Ready Boston initiatives, including an Infrastructure Coordination Committee, a Climate Research Advisory Organization, and governance structures for district-scale coastal flood protection.

**Citations**

- Governance for a Changing Climate: Adapting Boston's Built Environment for Increased Flooding (p. 12)

## Question 6

**Question:** How are 'Voices that Matter' and 'Views that Matter' different in the kind of evidence or perspective they provide?

**Answer:** Views that Matter is primarily a survey-based report that captures opinions from a large Greater Boston sample. Voices that Matter follows up with focus groups involving 70 Boston-area residents of color, adding qualitative detail and lived experience to those survey findings.

**Citations**

- Views that Matter: Race and Opinions on Climate Change of Boston Area Residents (p. 7)
- Voices that Matter: Boston Area Residents of Color Discuss Climate Change (p. 28)

## Question 7

**Question:** What concerns does the East Boston resilience work raise about climate adaptation, gentrification, and displacement?

**Answer:** The East Boston report warns that climate resilience efforts can fuel gentrification, displacement, and loss of community trust if they are not designed equitably. It frames climate adaptation as inseparable from housing pressure, neighborhood change, and longstanding local inequities.

**Citations**

- Opportunity in the Complexity: Recommendations for Equitable Climate Resilience in East Boston (p. 44)

## Question 8

**Question:** What does the SSL report say about the Massachusetts Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Program in the Greater Boston region?

**Answer:** The MVP report describes the program as a state effort that supports municipalities in identifying vulnerabilities and planning climate adaptation. It also highlights lessons about cross-sector collaboration and argues that social equity and climate justice need to be more fully integrated into resilience planning.

**Citations**

- Learning from the Massachusetts Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Program in the Greater Boston Region (p. 12)

## Question 9

**Question:** What has SSL done about climate resilience in Boston?

**Answer:** SSL has built a body of climate resilience work in Boston that includes research on vulnerable communities, survey and focus-group work with Boston-area residents of color, and policy-focused reports on governance, financing, and equitable resilience planning such as the East Boston work.

**Citations**

- [https://www.umb.edu/ssl/](https://www.umb.edu/ssl/)
- Views that Matter: Race and Opinions on Climate Change of Boston Area Residents (p. 16)
- Governance for a Changing Climate: Adapting Boston's Built Environment for Increased Flooding (p. 52)
